## Summary

This study examined whether the normalized pairwise variability index calculated from inter-vowel-onset intervals (nPVI-Vo) can characterize the local temporal organization of Japanese speech across different speaking contexts. Recordings from 16 speakers in the Corpus of Spontaneous Japanese were compared across presentation-like speech, dialogue, and rereading.

Presentation-like speech had a higher speaking rate than rereading, whereas dialogue exhibited greater local temporal variability (higher nPVI-Vo) than either presentation-like speech or rereading. Speaking rate and nPVI-Vo were not clearly correlated. In contrast, the conventional nPVI calculated from vowel durations did not clearly distinguish the three contexts in pairwise comparisons.

These findings suggest that vowel-onset-based nPVI provides a candidate acoustic measure of local speech timing that complements overall speaking rate. The study did not directly assess presentation quality, intelligibility, or comprehensibility.

From an HCI perspective, the results offer a measurement foundation for speech analytics and technology-assisted presentation training. Future interfaces could examine speech rate and local timing variability as distinct observable features, while separately evaluating whether these measures are informative for feedback or presentation outcomes.

## Research line

- **Measuring**: operationalizing local temporal organization through vowel-onset intervals and nPVI
- **Speech Analysis**: comparing presentation-like speech, dialogue, and rereading using corpus-based acoustic measures
- **HCI**: identifying objective speech features that could be incorporated into interactive presentation-support tools
- **Presentation Skills**: extending measurement beyond speaking rate without presuming that rhythmic variation indexes presentation quality
